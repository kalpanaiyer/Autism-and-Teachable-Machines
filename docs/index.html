Autism and Teachable Machines

Introduction

This page includes the materials derived from a user study on making teachable interfaces inclusive for students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The study examined how secondary school students (grades 7-12, ages 12-21), including students with level 1 ASD and typically developing (TD) peers, train Google's Teachable Machine to recognize objects, and how this affects their knowledge and perceptions of machine learning. The materials include the questionnaires used in the study: pre- and post-study questionnaires on machine learning knowledge and perceptions, and a social validity questionnaire on attitudes toward the teachable interface. They also cover the study procedures for the on-campus session and the at-home generalization task, as well as the control condition, in which TD students watched the video tutorial but did not use the interface.

Study Procedures

Participants

We recruited secondary school students (grades 7-12, ages 12-21) from the local community through e-mail listservs of educational centers, autism organizations, and parent/advocacy organizations, and through flyers posted in campus buildings. Parents contacted the research team, and eligibility was confirmed through an e-mail screening. Parents of students with ASD obtained diagnosis and level information from their child's medical and educational records. Participants were matched across groups by grade level, and each participated individually.

Eligibility criteria

  • Students with ASD:
    • Diagnosis of ASD level 1 per DSM-5, with no co-occurring intellectual disability
    • Average or above-average IQ (per participant or parent report) and majority of the school day spent in a general education classroom
    • No prior experience with Google's Teachable Machine
    • Secondary school grades (7-12), ages 12-21
    • No visual or hearing impairments
  • Typically developing (TD) students:
    • No diagnosis of a disability that qualifies for special education services
    • No prior experience with Google's Teachable Machine
    • Secondary school grades (7-12), ages 12-21
    • No visual or hearing impairments

Groups and sample size

Participants were assigned to one of three groups: Intervention (ASD), Intervention (TD), and Control (TD). TD students were randomly selected for the control group. We targeted about 12-18 participants per group (about 36-54 in total). A power analysis indicated that about 15 participants per group were needed to detect a medium effect (0.75) with power of 0.8 at a significance level of 0.05. Stimuli

Teachable interface. Participants used Google's Teachable Machine (teachablemachine.withgoogle.com), a browser-based application in which users provide training examples and see the model's predictions in real time.

Video tutorial. All participants, including the control group, watched a tutorial on Teachable Machine in the web browser. [Add link or description of the exact video used.]

On-campus training objects. For the play tasks, the research team selected or created physical stimuli for participants to teach the interface to distinguish. Examples included different fruits and origami shapes and colors.

At-home objects. For the generalization task, participants chose their own objects or images to train the interface. No stimuli were provided by the research team.

Collection Process

Participants completed the study in two parts: an on-campus session and, for the intervention groups, an at-home generalization task.

  1. Session 1 (On-campus): After participants provided assent or consent and their parents provided permission, the research team held a brief warm-up introduction with each participant, with light snacks provided. Participants then completed a demographic questionnaire (age, grade level, gender, ethnicity, and SES), a pre-study questionnaire on their experience with and knowledge of machine learning concepts, and watched the video tutorial. Control participants then completed the post-study questionnaire, and their session lasted up to 45 minutes. Intervention participants continued with the following activities, and their session lasted up to 1 hour and 30 minutes:
    • Play tasks: Participants used the teachable interface to train a model to distinguish the stimuli objects.
    • Demonstration: Participants showed how they trained the interface and discussed their experience, including how well it worked and where it became confused.
    • Post-study and social validity questionnaires: Participants answered questions about their knowledge and perception of machine learning concepts and their attitudes toward the teachable interface.

    Each participant completed the session individually in a laboratory setting, and parents did not assist with study tasks. All sessions were video recorded.

  2. Session 2 (At-home generalization): Intervention participants used the teachable interface at home for 20-30 minutes with objects or images of their choice. Parents used a screen recorder to capture the interaction, sent the recording to the research team, and answered social validity questions about their child's level of involvement. Parents were asked not to influence the child's performance.

Participants received a $25 cash incentive after completing the on-campus session. Intervention participants who also submitted the home recording and parent responses received an additional $15 Amazon e-gift card.
